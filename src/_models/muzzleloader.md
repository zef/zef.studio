Loading a muzzleloader in the field means measuring powder from a flask into a
measure, pouring the measure down the barrel, seating a bullet, and finding a
primer. That is four objects and two hands, in the cold, usually while trying
not to lose any of it. This kit reduces the whole reload to one thing you pull
out of a pocket.

The tube is cut to hold exactly one charge by volume, which is how black powder
is measured — *grains* here means the volume that many grains of reference
powder occupies, not what the charge weighs. Substitutes are less dense, so the
same volume weighs less on a scale, which is why the tube is sized in cubic
millimetres per grain and why that number is adjustable.

Two details make it work at the muzzle. The neck tapers so it funnels powder
straight down the bore rather than spilling it around the crown, and the tube
below the neck is deliberately fatter than the bore, so it catches on the muzzle
instead of following the charge in. The model refuses to build if those two
conditions ever contradict each other.

The cap carries the primer, so the charge and its ignition travel together. Its
pocket is bored from the outside face, which means the cap prints face-down and
the pocket comes out as a hole in the bed-side surface — no supports, and a
crisp bore for the primer to grip.
