Every shop accumulates hoses that do not match. This is the part that makes
them match: a barb on each end, a collar in the middle to stop the hose at the
right depth, and independent diameters, so the same model covers both a splice
and a reducer.

Size the barb to the hose's *inner* bore and go a few tenths over — the ribs
need something to bite into. If the joint will hold pressure, print it in PETG
with four or five perimeters and no infill, so the walls fuse into one solid
tube rather than a shell around a lattice.

It prints standing up. That puts the layer lines across the direction the hose
pulls, which is the strong way round for this part.
